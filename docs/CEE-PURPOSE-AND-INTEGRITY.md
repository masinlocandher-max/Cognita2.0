# Cognita 2.0 — CEE Purpose and Integrity Rationale

Status: Canonical working copy for Cognita 2.0
Updated: 2026-09-28

This document explains why the Cognita Entrance Examination exists, why honest answers matter, and how the CEE should be presented to applicants across the public website, admissions flow, exam invitation, and pre-exam integrity screen. The CEE is academically coded **AI-00** and functions as Cognita's diagnostic decision instrument for admission and training placement.

## Why the CEE matters

The Cognita Entrance Examination is an important part of how we understand each applicant before learning begins.

It is not designed simply to determine who is “good enough” to enter Cognita. It helps us understand where a learner is already strong, where additional support may be needed, and which learning pathway is most appropriate.

Every learner comes to Cognita with a different background.

Some may already have strong communication and AI skills. Others may have strong potential but need more support in English, research, digital literacy, or foundational AI concepts. Treating every learner exactly the same would ignore those differences.

The CEE helps us make a more informed academic decision.

It allows Cognita to identify whether a learner should receive:

- **Foundation Required** — substantial support in one or more diagnosed competency domains;
- **Foundation Accelerated** — only the targeted foundation modules linked to demonstrated gaps;
- or **Direct Track Entry** — progression without repeating foundation competencies already demonstrated.

AI-00 is the assessment itself. Foundation training is delivered through separately coded foundation modules rather than treating AI-00 as a course.

Our goal is not to make the examination unnecessarily difficult.

Our goal is to place learners where they have the strongest opportunity to learn, progress, and succeed.

## Why honest answers matter

We encourage every applicant to answer the CEE wholeheartedly and independently.

Trying to produce a higher score through unauthorized assistance may create the appearance of readiness, but it can ultimately place the learner in a program that does not match their actual needs.

That does not help the learner, and it does not help Cognita teach them well.

An honest result gives us something far more useful than a perfect result:

**an accurate picture of your current readiness.**

If the CEE shows that you need additional support, that is not automatically a negative result. It gives Cognita the information needed to recommend a more appropriate starting point.

If the CEE shows that you are ready for more advanced learning, we also want that decision to be based on your own demonstrated ability.

This is why integrity matters from the very beginning.

## The CEE is part of the learning journey

At Cognita, assessment is not only about producing a score.

Assessment helps us make better decisions about learning.

The CEE helps answer questions such as:

- Can the learner understand and follow complex instructions?
- Can the learner communicate ideas clearly?
- Can the learner reason with practical numbers, constraints, and uncertainty?
- Can the learner work safely and reliably in a digital environment?
- Does the learner understand basic AI concepts, limitations, risks, and human responsibility?
- Can the learner recognize when information needs to be verified?
- Can the learner evaluate sources and evidence?
- Can the learner design or critique an AI-assisted workflow?
- What specific support would help this learner progress successfully?

These questions matter because Cognita is built around demonstrated competence, not passive completion.

## Do your own work

Applicants should complete the CEE without unauthorized assistance from another person, generative AI system, answer-sharing service, or other prohibited resource.

The examination includes integrity safeguards because the result affects academic placement and admission decisions.

We ask applicants to approach the CEE with seriousness, but not fear.

Do your best.

Answer what you genuinely know.

Think carefully when you are uncertain.

Do not try to create a result that does not represent your actual ability.

A truthful assessment gives Cognita the best opportunity to support your development.

## What Cognita values

Cognita does not expect every applicant to know everything before entering.

We value:

- honesty;
- willingness to learn;
- independent thinking;
- responsible use of technology;
- curiosity;
- effort;
- and the ability to improve.

A learner who answers honestly gives us a meaningful starting point.

That is far more valuable than a score achieved through assistance that does not reflect the learner’s true readiness.

## Core principle

**The CEE is not designed to catch learners out. It is designed to understand them accurately.**

Your result helps us decide where your Cognita learning journey should begin.

That is why we encourage every applicant to take the examination seriously, answer wholeheartedly, and complete it with integrity.

## Required presentation rule

The CEE experience should communicate purpose before enforcement.

Before the integrity pledge or exam start action, applicants should understand:

1. why the CEE exists;
2. how the result may affect both admission and academic placement;
3. why honest answers are more useful than artificially high scores;
4. that needing foundational support is not automatically a failure;
5. that cheating can result in an inaccurate pathway recommendation;
6. that Cognita values honesty, effort, independent judgment, and willingness to learn.

Recommended short statement immediately before the integrity pledge:

> **The CEE is not designed to catch you out. It is designed to understand your current readiness accurately. Answering independently helps us recommend the learning pathway that can support you best.**

## Tone rule

CEE integrity language must be serious and clear without sounding threatening, punitive, or adversarial.

The institution should explain that integrity safeguards protect:

- the learner from being placed above their actual readiness;
- other applicants from unfair assessment conditions;
- Cognita’s academic standards;
- the credibility of future credentials.

The message should encourage applicants to give their genuine best effort rather than chase a perfect score.

## Relationship to the Cognita 2.0 source of truth

This document extends the CEE role defined in `docs/COGNITA-2.0-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`.

The current implementation preserves:

- the 70-minute persistent CEE session;
- invitation-only access;
- 100 total points;
- objective and applied-response evidence;
- evaluator review requirement;
- integrity-event logging;
- separation of admission from training placement;
- competency-profile reporting rather than reliance on total score alone;
- and the rule that exact final institutional cut scores remain subject to formal approval.

The detailed v1.2 structure is defined in `docs/CEE-AI00-V1.2-ASSESSMENT-BLUEPRINT.md`.
