## 2. Institutional classification and legal positioning

### 2.1 Cognita’s intended position

Cognita 2.0 is intended to operate as a **private, non-degree continuing and professional learning provider**. It provides structured training, assessment, projects, and private institutional credentials.

Cognita is not to present itself as:

- a CHED-recognized college or university;
- a degree-granting higher education institution unless separately authorized;
- a TESDA-registered TVI unless the institution/program has actually been registered;
- a provider of TESDA National Certificates or Certificates of Competency unless formally authorized;
- a PRC licensure provider or licensure-qualifying school unless formally recognized for that purpose.

### 2.2 Regulatory boundary that must be resolved before real intake

TESDA states that registration under UTPRAS is mandatory for TVET programs and that a TVET institution must comply with registration requirements before offering a program or accepting enrollees. TESDA also states that registration is compulsory for TVET programs with or without promulgated Training Regulations.

**Operational rule:** Cognita must not simultaneously characterize a Day-1 program as a formal TVET program and operate it as an unregistered TVET program. Before accepting real enrollees, legal/compliance review must determine whether each Cognita offering falls within a regulated TVET category requiring TESDA program registration.

Until that classification is resolved, public copy should use conservative language such as:

> Cognita Institute is a private, non-degree training and learning institution. Cognita credentials are private institutional credentials and are not CHED degrees, TESDA National Certificates, TESDA Certificates of Competency, or PRC licenses unless explicitly stated and independently verifiable.

### 2.3 Consumer-protection rule

Marketing must not represent that a service has sponsorship, approval, characteristics, recognition, or benefits that it does not actually have. Claims such as “accredited,” “government-recognized,” “TESDA-certified,” “CHED-recognized,” “licensed,” “guaranteed employment,” or “qualifies you for PRC licensure” require verifiable authority and must not be implied through design, credential naming, seals, or copy.

### 2.4 Accessibility and non-discrimination

Cognita must not use a vague “physical/mental fitness” requirement as a general admission barrier. Admission should be based on the academic/technical requirements of the program, with reasonable accommodation where feasible. Disability must not by itself be treated as a reason to deny admission.

### 2.5 Privacy principle

Personal data must be collected for declared, specific, legitimate purposes, limited to what is necessary, protected appropriately, and retained only as long as necessary for the declared purpose, legal claims, legitimate business purposes, or another lawful basis. Cognita must not collect high-risk biometric data such as facial-recognition attendance data merely for convenience.

## 4. Admissions eligibility — Day-1 provisional recommendation

### 4.1 Age

**PROVISIONAL recommendation:** Day-1 public intake is limited to applicants **18 years old or older**.

Reason: this avoids introducing a half-designed minor consent, safeguarding, contracting, payment, data-processing, and parent/guardian workflow into the first cohort. A future minor-admissions policy may be introduced separately.

### 4.2 Education

No college degree is required.

Recommended readiness standard:

- completion of senior high school, high school, ALS equivalent, or another educational background reasonably sufficient for the program; or
- an alternative admission decision based on demonstrated readiness, work experience, or CEE performance where the Academic team approves it.

Cognita should avoid excluding capable adult learners solely because a formal diploma is difficult to produce unless a specific program legally or academically requires it.

### 4.3 Language

Instruction is primarily in English. Applicants must demonstrate sufficient functional English to understand instructions and complete required work, with AI-00 communication support available where appropriate.

### 4.4 Technology

Applicants need regular access to:

- a reliable internet connection;
- a modern browser;
- a computer or tablet suitable for required work;
- microphone/camera access only when genuinely required for guided sessions or an approved assessment process.

A smartphone may support portions of learning but should not be represented as sufficient for every assignment if the program requires desktop-class tasks.

### 4.5 Identity and documents

Collect only what is necessary for admission, identity verification, credential issuance, or lawful operations.

Day-1 document requirements should be limited to the minimum necessary. Government ID verification may be required before credential issuance or where needed for identity assurance. Educational documents should not be collected merely because other schools collect them.

### 4.6 Grounds for non-approval or deferral

Examples:

- materially incomplete application;
- false or fraudulent information;
- inability to meet essential program requirements even with reasonable accommodation;
- CEE performance that indicates the applicant is not yet ready for the available pathway;
- unresolved academic-integrity concerns in the admissions process;
- lack of a currently appropriate program/pathway.

Admissions decisions must not be based on protected characteristics or disability itself.

## 5. Cognita Entrance Examination (CEE)

### 5.1 Purpose

**LOCKED:** The CEE exists to understand the applicant accurately before learning begins.

It helps Cognita identify:

- current communication readiness;
- AI foundations;
- research and verification judgment;
- applied reasoning and instruction quality;
- whether foundational support is needed;
- whether a guided or self-paced route may be more appropriate.

The CEE is not designed mainly to catch applicants out. Honest performance is more useful than an artificially high score because inaccurate performance can lead to inaccurate placement.

See `docs/CEE-PURPOSE-AND-INTEGRITY.md`.

### 5.2 Current CEE structure

**Total:** 100 points  
**Time:** 70 minutes  
**Final decision:** Human evaluator review required

| Section | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Functional English & Communication | 30 |
| AI Foundations | 25 |
| Research & Verification Judgment | 15 |
| Applied Communication & AI Evaluation | 30 |
| **Total** | **100** |

The first 70 points are objectively scored in the current frontend model. The final 30 points are applied written tasks requiring human evaluation.

### 5.3 Integrity controls

Current/approved principles:

- invitation-only access;
- one persistent timed attempt;
- refreshing does not reset the timer;
- integrity acknowledgement before start;
- window-leave/paste or similar events may be logged as signals;
- time expiry submits the current attempt;
- objective score does not equal final admission decision;
- applied responses and integrity context require evaluator review.

Production may later add stronger technical controls, but Cognita should not introduce invasive proctoring or biometric surveillance merely because it is technically possible. Any production proctoring method requires a privacy, proportionality, accessibility, and security review.

### 5.4 Provisional admission and placement bands

These are **pilot thresholds, not LOCKED policy**:

| Final reviewed score | Provisional academic interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 85–100 | Strong readiness; Direct Track Entry may be recommended if no critical domain is weak |
| 75–84 | Admissible; Direct Entry or Foundation Accelerated depending on domain scores/evaluator review |
| 60–74 | Conditional readiness; Foundation Required/Targeted Bridge normally recommended |
| Below 60 | Not yet ready for current intake; remediation and reapplication guidance |

Additional provisional rule: a high total score does not override a severe weakness in a critical domain. Evaluators may require targeted support even when the overall score is high.

### 5.5 Applied-response rubric — provisional

Each 15-point applied task may be judged on:

- understanding of the task;
- clarity and structure;
- reasoning and judgment;
- evidence/verification awareness;
- constraints and risk awareness;
- independent explanation.

The evaluator must record a short rationale for the score.

### 5.6 Retake/reapplication — provisional

Recommended Day-1 policy:

- no immediate self-service retake;
- an applicant who is not admitted may reapply after a minimum **30-day remediation period**;
- a new CEE invitation requires a new admissions review;
- no retake fee is approved until the commercial model is approved;
- exceptional technical failures may result in attempt invalidation and a replacement attempt without treating the event as an academic retake.

### 5.7 CEE appeal — provisional

Applicants may request review for:

- scoring/data-entry error;
- technical submission failure;
- documented procedural irregularity.

An appeal is not an entitlement to negotiate academic judgment or obtain question answers.

Recommended window: within 7 calendar days of the decision. Target response: within 10 business days.

## 6. Cognita Professional AI Program — 10-week curriculum v1 draft

**Status:** PROVISIONAL curriculum for academic review and pilot testing.

### Week 1 — AI Foundations, Responsibility, and Learner Purpose

**Outcomes**

Learner can:

- explain generative AI in practical terms;
- distinguish assistance from human responsibility;
- identify common strengths, limitations, and hallucination risk;
- define a personal/professional AI-use objective.

**Learning activities**

- AI reality-check lesson;
- capability/limitation scenarios;
- responsible-use discussion;
- baseline reflection.

**Required output**

- Cognita AI Purpose & Use Map.

### Week 2 — Prompt Strategy, Problem Framing, and Verification

**Outcomes**

Learner can:

- define objective, audience, context, constraints, inputs, and success criteria;
- write structured prompts;
- improve weak prompts iteratively;
- identify claims that require verification.

**Required outputs**

- Prompt Portfolio;
- verification exercise with source log.

### Week 3 — Research, Communication, and Digital Efficiency

**Outcomes**

Learner can:

- research with AI without treating AI as the source of truth;
- compare source authority, recency, and relevance;
- edit AI-assisted writing for clarity, tone, and factual integrity;
- organize files/workflows efficiently.

**Required outputs**

- Research Brief;
- before/after professional communication revision;
- personal digital-workspace checklist.

### Week 4 — Workflow Design, Tool Orientation, and Foundation Gate

**Outcomes**

Learner can:

- select an appropriate AI system/tool for a task;
- divide complex work into human/AI stages;
- define approval and verification checkpoints;
- build a repeatable SOP.

**Required outputs**

- Workflow Blueprint;
- Personal SOP;
- Foundation Competency Check.

**Foundation Gate**

Progression is based on demonstrated readiness, not mere attendance. Students who do not meet the gate receive remediation before advanced specialization.

### Week 5 — Specialization Core I

Learner enters one specialization.

**AI for Students:** research, study design, academic integrity, knowledge synthesis.  
**AI for Creatives:** creative briefing, ideation, visual/content workflows, human creative direction.  
**AI for Entrepreneurs:** customer/problem research, market validation, business use cases, offer clarity.  
**AI for Professionals & VAs:** client communication, productivity, research, documentation, service workflows.

**Required output:** Track Output 1.

### Week 6 — Specialization Core II

Focus: quality, repeatability, professional standards, and revision.

**Required output:** Track Output 2 plus evaluator feedback/revision.

### Week 7 — Applied Workflow and Professional Delivery

Learner builds a multi-stage AI-assisted workflow relevant to the specialization, with documented human checkpoints and quality controls.

**Required output:** Track Workflow Project.

### Week 8 — Portfolio Project and Evidence of Competence

Learner completes a larger track-specific applied project and documents:

- objective;
- process;
- AI role;
- human role;
- evidence used;
- revisions;
- limitations;
- final result.

**Required output:** Portfolio Project.

### Week 9 — Capstone Planning and Build

Learner defines a real or realistic problem and submits:

- capstone brief;
- success criteria;
- evidence plan;
- workflow plan;
- risk/ethics considerations;
- draft/prototype.

Facilitator gives structured feedback before finalization.

### Week 10 — Capstone Completion, Defense, and Reflection

Learner submits and defends the final capstone.

Final requirements:

- final professional output;
- source/evidence record where relevant;
- AI-use disclosure;
- revision history or rationale;
- oral or written professional defense;
- final reflection on capability and limitations.

A student does not pass the capstone merely because the artifact looks polished. The learner must be able to explain and defend the work.

## 9. Assessment and grading architecture

### 9.1 Assessment types

Cognita may use:

- low-stakes quizzes/checks;
- applied exercises;
- research/verification tasks;
- professional outputs;
- revision assignments;
- track projects;
- capstone;
- oral/written defense;
- final competency checks.

### 9.2 Competency rubric dimensions

Major work should be evaluated against dimensions such as:

1. **Understanding & Accuracy**
2. **Reasoning & Judgment**
3. **Evidence & Verification**
4. **Application & Execution**
5. **Communication & Clarity**
6. **Responsible AI Use & Integrity**
7. **Reflection & Defense** where applicable

### 9.3 Provisional mastery scale

| Score | Descriptor | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 90–100 | Exemplary | Consistently exceeds the required standard with strong judgment and execution |
| 80–89 | Proficient | Meets the required professional/academic standard |
| 70–79 | Developing | Partial competence; revision/remediation may be required |
| Below 70 | Not Yet Competent | Required competence not yet demonstrated |

Cognita should prefer mastery language over an A–F school-like system unless an A–F scale is later shown to be operationally useful.

### 9.4 Provisional program completion standard

Recommended for pilot:

- overall weighted score of **75% or higher**;
- all required outputs submitted;
- no critical competency left below 70 after permitted remediation;
- capstone rated at least **Proficient (80%)** overall;
- professional defense completed where required;
- academic-integrity requirements satisfied;
- guided attendance requirement satisfied where applicable.

### 9.5 Distinction — provisional

**With Distinction** may be awarded when:

- final weighted score is 90% or higher;
- capstone is 90% or higher;
- no major assessment was passed only through capped remediation;
- no substantiated academic-integrity violation occurred.

## 10. Academic standing, remediation, and reassessment

### 10.1 Statuses

- **Good Standing** — meeting current progression requirements.
- **Support Required** — learner is at risk and has assigned intervention.
- **Incomplete** — approved temporary missing requirement with a defined completion deadline.
- **Remediation** — learner must revise/relearn specified competencies before progressing.
- **Withdrawn** — learner exits voluntarily or is administratively withdrawn after process.
- **Not Completed** — final requirements were not met within the permitted period.
- **Completed** — all program requirements satisfied.
- **Completed With Distinction** — distinction criteria satisfied.

Avoid stigmatizing labels such as “failing student” in learner-facing systems.

### 10.2 Remediation — provisional

A remediation plan may include:

- targeted lesson review;
- evaluator feedback conference;
- resubmission;
- alternate practice task assessing the same competency;
- additional verification/research exercise;
- structured tutoring/support.

### 10.3 Reassessment — provisional

One reassessment may be permitted for a failed major assessment when the Academic team determines that reassessment is academically appropriate.

Rather than an arbitrary punitive grade cap, Cognita should record that the standard was achieved through reassessment. If a cap is later adopted, it must be justified and consistently applied.

## 11. Academic integrity and AI-use policy

Cognita teaches AI. Therefore the integrity policy must distinguish **learning with AI** from **outsourcing the learner’s competence to AI**.

### 11.1 AI use generally permitted without special disclosure

When the assessment is not testing the corrected skill itself:

- spellcheck;
- grammar correction;
- accessibility tools;
- formatting assistance;
- approved transcription;
- basic productivity functions.

### 11.2 AI use permitted with disclosure

Depending on assignment rules:

- brainstorming;
- outlining;
- prompt testing;
- research planning;
- draft generation;
- code skeletons;
- visual ideation;
- workflow design;
- analysis assistance.

Learner must remain able to explain, verify, revise, and defend the submission.

Recommended disclosure fields:

- tools used;
- tasks supported by AI;
- work completed independently;
- AI output rejected/revised;
- facts independently verified;
- sensitive data protection;
- intellectual-property considerations;
- final human decision owner.

### 11.3 AI prohibited

Unless an assessment explicitly says otherwise:

- CEE objective/applied responses;
- closed-book quizzes testing foundational knowledge;
- supervised competency checks designated as independent work;
- any task where the stated purpose is to measure unaided learner performance.

### 11.4 Capstone rule

AI is **not automatically prohibited** from Cognita capstones. That would conflict with an applied-AI institution.

Instead:

- use must follow the capstone brief;
- AI assistance must be disclosed where required;
- evidence and claims must be verified;
- the learner must make and defend final decisions;
- the learner may not submit an AI-produced artifact they cannot explain or revise;
- confidential/personal data may not be entered into unauthorized AI services.

### 11.5 Misconduct

Potential misconduct includes:

- plagiarism;
- unauthorized AI use;
- impersonation;
- account sharing;
- collusion where independent work is required;
- fabricated sources/data;
- falsified records;
- evasion of assessment integrity controls.

Discipline must be proportionate and documented. A serious allegation should not automatically result in permanent exclusion without review and an opportunity to respond.

## 12. Guided attendance and participation

**Status:** PROVISIONAL.

Recommended Day-1 requirement: **80% minimum attendance in required live/synchronous sessions** for the guided program.

Attendance is one component of participation, not proof of competence.

### Rules

- attendance should be recorded through normal session check-in/platform records and instructor confirmation;
- do not deploy facial recognition for attendance;
- repeated lateness/early departure may be recorded, but rigid “three tardies equals one absence” arithmetic is optional and should not override reasonable judgment;
- illness, bereavement, emergencies, accessibility needs, work emergencies, and connectivity disruptions may qualify for excused absence with reasonable evidence where needed;
- makeup work may restore missed learning but does not automatically rewrite attendance history;
- three consecutive unexcused absences trigger an academic-support/continuation review, not automatic expulsion without process;
- falling below the attendance minimum triggers intervention and may make the learner ineligible for completion if required learning cannot be reasonably recovered.

## 13. Credentials taxonomy

Cognita issues private institutional credentials only unless a future external recognition is formally obtained.

### 13.1 Day-1 recommended credential

**Cognita Certificate of Completion**

Issued when the learner satisfies the approved completion requirements of the named program.

Optional notation:

- **With Distinction**

### 13.2 Non-completer documentation

Do not dilute the credential system by automatically issuing a “Certificate of Participation” to every incomplete learner.

Where appropriate, Cognita may issue a **Participation Record** or **Statement of Attendance** clearly stating that it is not a completion credential.

### 13.3 Reserved/avoid terms

Do not use credential names that can be confused with government-issued qualifications, including **Certificate of Competency**, unless legal review confirms the exact wording cannot mislead and the context is unmistakable. TESDA already uses “Certificate of Competency (CoC)” as an official credential term.

### 13.4 Credential content

A Cognita credential should include:

- learner name;
- program name;
- completion date;
- unique credential ID;
- issuer;
- authorized signatory/signatories;
- verification method when production registry exists;
- clear private/non-degree wording where needed to prevent confusion.

No certificate should imply academic credit, government qualification level, national certification, licensure, or degree equivalency unless formally authorized.

### 21.1 Record types

Production systems may hold:

- application record;
- admissions decision;
- CEE attempt/result;

- enrollment/transaction record;
- attendance/progress;
- assessments and evaluator decisions;

- completion decision;
- credential record;

### 21.2 Cognita Learning Record

Rather than implying government academic credit, Cognita may issue a **Cognita Learning Record** listing:

- program;
- modules/competencies;
- completion status;
- approved result descriptors/scores;
- completion date;
- credential ID.

Do not use “credit units” unless Cognita has an approved internal definition that cannot be mistaken for CHED academic credits.

### 21.3 Verification

Future production credentials should use a unique ID and a verification page/registry. A QR code may point to that page.

The public verification response should reveal only information necessary to verify the credential and should not expose the learner’s full academic file.

### 21.4 Retention — provisional schedule for privacy review

The Data Privacy Act does not provide one universal retention period for every Cognita record. Retention must be purpose-based.

Recommended starting schedule for legal/privacy review:

- unsuccessful/incomplete applications: 2 years after final action;
- CEE attempt and admissions evidence: 2 years after final action, longer only if needed for dispute/legal basis;
- active student academic records: through enrollment plus defined post-completion period;
- detailed submissions/feedback: 5 years after completion/withdrawal unless a shorter period is sufficient;
- financial records: according to applicable tax/accounting/legal retention requirements;
- minimal credential verification register: long-term/indefinite only to the extent necessary to verify issued credentials, using minimized fields;
- support logs and incidental communications: purpose-specific shorter periods where possible.

No record should be kept forever merely because it might be useful someday.

## 26. Official legal/regulatory reference points

These sources are used as compliance anchors for this provisional document. Formal launch policy should still receive professional legal/compliance review.

1. **TESDA — Program Registration and Accreditation / UTPRAS**  
   TESDA states that UTPRAS registration is mandatory for TVET programs and that institutions must comply before offering a TVET program.

2. **TESDA — UTPRAS Guidelines**  
   TESDA states that a school offering a new TVET program must register prior to accepting enrollees and that registration is compulsory with or without Training Regulations.

3. **Commission on Higher Education — RA 7722 mandate / CHED mandate**  
   CHED’s mandate concerns higher education institutions and higher education programs. Cognita must not imply CHED status it does not possess.

4. **Republic Act No. 10173 — Data Privacy Act of 2012, National Privacy Commission**  
   Personal information must be collected for legitimate purposes, be proportionate, and be retained only as long as necessary or otherwise lawfully justified.

5. **Data Privacy Act Implementing Rules and Regulations — National Privacy Commission**  
   Personal data must not be retained longer than necessary and should not be kept in perpetuity for an undefined future use.

6. **Republic Act No. 7277 — Magna Carta for Disabled Persons, education provisions**  
   Learning institutions may not deny admission to a course by reason of disability alone and should take special needs into account.

7. **Republic Act No. 7394 — Consumer Act of the Philippines**  
   Deceptive representations regarding approval, sponsorship, quality, characteristics, or benefits of services are prohibited.
