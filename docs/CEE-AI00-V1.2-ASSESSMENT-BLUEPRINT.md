# Cognita Entrance Examination / AI-00 v1.2 — Diagnostic Assessment Blueprint

**Status:** Working implementation for academic review and pilot validation  
**Date:** 2026-09-28  
**Instrument:** Cognita Entrance Examination (CEE)  
**Academic code:** AI-00  
**Time:** 70 minutes  
**Total:** 100 points

## 1. Purpose

AI-00 / CEE is Cognita's diagnostic admissions and readiness assessment.

Its primary academic purpose is to determine **where training should begin**, not merely to rank applicants by one total score.

The assessment must produce two separate decisions:

1. **Admission decision** — human-reviewed determination of whether the learner can enter the current Cognita intake.
2. **Training placement** — competency-based recommendation for Direct Track Entry, Foundation Accelerated, or Foundation Required.

A learner may be admitted while still being assigned foundation modules.

The total score is useful, but it must never hide a serious weakness in an individual competency domain.

## 2. Assessment design principle

AI-00 uses evidence-centred assessment logic:

- define the capability Cognita needs to understand;
- identify observable evidence that supports a claim about that capability;
- use tasks that generate that evidence;
- score the evidence consistently;
- route training from the resulting competency profile.

The assessment should measure practical readiness rather than trivia, memorized terminology, tool-brand familiarity, or socioeconomic exposure.

## 3. Diagnostic domains

### Objective assessment — 70 points

| Domain | Items | Points | Training link |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Communication & Instruction Comprehension | 10 | 15 | FND-01 |
| Reasoning, Numeracy & Practical Problem Solving | 8 | 15 | FND-02 |
| Digital & Information Literacy | 7 | 10 | FND-03 |
| AI Foundations, Safety & Responsible Use | 10 | 15 | FND-04 |
| Research, Verification & Evidence Judgment | 10 | 15 | FND-05 |
| **Objective total** | **45** | **70** | |

### Applied assessment — 30 points

Two human-scored tasks, 15 points each:

1. **Applied Instruction & Workflow Design**
2. **AI Evidence & Decision Audit**

Weak applied performance may trigger **FND-06 Applied Prompting, Workflow & Decision Practice** even when objective scores are otherwise strong.

## 4. What each domain must establish

### 4.1 Communication & Instruction Comprehension

The learner can:

- understand conditional and multi-step instructions;
- identify ambiguity;
- communicate constraints and risk clearly;
- adjust language to audience without changing facts;
- create precise instructions;
- distinguish required information from invented detail.

This is not an English-literature test. Grammar is assessed only where it affects functional communication.

### 4.2 Reasoning, Numeracy & Practical Problem Solving

The learner can:

- interpret percentages and basic quantitative changes;
- separate sequence from causation;
- identify missing decision evidence;
- reason under uncertainty;
- compare alternatives against explicit criteria;
- detect conflicts between authorized values and requested actions.

This is practical numeracy and judgment, not advanced mathematics.

### 4.3 Digital & Information Literacy

The learner can:

- organize and manage digital files reliably;
- use permissions and least-privilege access;
- recognize common digital-security risks;
- check formulas and data before reliance;
- select tools based on task, data, format, and reliability;
- handle personal or sensitive information appropriately;
- find current authoritative digital information.

### 4.4 AI Foundations, Safety & Responsible Use

The learner can:

- distinguish fluency from factual reliability;
- recognize hallucinations and fabricated citations;
- understand probabilistic output;
- identify prompt-injection risk;
- use human approval for consequential actions;
- evaluate AI outputs against task-specific criteria;
- identify bias risk;
- explain appropriate human accountability.

AI competence is not measured by knowing brand names or fashionable prompt vocabulary.

### 4.5 Research, Verification & Evidence Judgment

The learner can:

- distinguish primary, authoritative, and derivative sources;
- account for recency and effective dates;
- identify sampling and generalization problems;
- distinguish correlation or sequence from causation;
- investigate conflicting evidence;
- trace repeated claims back to their origin;
- inspect charts, scales, and underlying values critically.

### 4.6 Applied Judgment

The learner can use multiple competencies together in a realistic situation.

The applied tasks test whether the learner can:

- define scope;
- establish privacy and safety boundaries;
- design human-review checkpoints;
- identify unsupported claims;
- propose a verification plan;
- reason about operational and human risk;
- explain and defend a responsible decision process.

## 5. Applied-task rubric

### Task 1 — Applied Instruction & Workflow Design

Score each dimension 0–3:

1. Scope and instruction quality
2. Privacy and data boundaries
3. Escalation and human control
4. Verification and failure safeguards
5. Clarity and practical usability

Maximum: 15.

### Task 2 — AI Evidence & Decision Audit

Score each dimension 0–3:

1. Identification of unsupported claims
2. Evidence and source plan
3. Reasoning, causation, and generalization judgment
4. Operational and human-risk analysis
5. Quality of the recommended decision process

Maximum: 15.

Evaluator rationale is required.

## 6. Diagnostic reporting

The learner must have a **competency profile**, not only a total score.

Example:

- Communication & Instruction Comprehension — 84%
- Reasoning, Numeracy & Problem Solving — 63%
- Digital & Information Literacy — 86%
- AI Foundations & Responsible Use — 92%
- Research, Verification & Evidence — 58%
- Applied Judgment — evaluator-scored

The training system should use these domain results to assign only the support that the learner actually needs.

## 7. Provisional routing logic

These are diagnostic review lines for pilot use, not final institutional cut scores.

### Domain interpretation

- **85–100% — Strong readiness**
- **70–84% — Baseline ready**
- **50–69% — Targeted foundation indicated**
- **Below 50% — Foundation priority**

### Preliminary objective routing

**Direct Track Entry indicated**
- no objective domain below 70%.

**Foundation Accelerated indicated**
- one objective domain below 70%;
- no objective domain below 50%.

**Foundation Required indicated**
- any objective domain below 50%; or
- two or more objective domains below 70%.

The applied tasks and evaluator review may change the preliminary recommendation.

A high overall score must never erase a critical domain weakness.

## 8. Admission is separate from placement

AI-00 must not automatically turn every low diagnostic score into rejection.

An applicant may be admitted with required foundation training.

A not-admitted decision should require human review and an academically defensible reason, such as inability to meet minimum participation requirements after considering appropriate support, serious integrity concerns, or other approved admissions criteria.

Exact admission thresholds remain subject to formal academic approval.

## 9. Foundation mapping

- **FND-01** — Communication & Instruction Comprehension
- **FND-02** — Reasoning, Numeracy & Practical Problem Solving
- **FND-03** — Digital & Information Literacy
- **FND-04** — AI Foundations & Responsible Use
- **FND-05** — Research, Verification & Evidence
- **FND-06** — Applied Prompting, Workflow & Decision Practice

The learner should not repeat foundation competencies already demonstrated unless later evidence shows a genuine need.

## 10. Item-writing rules

Every scored item should:

- test one primary construct;
- use realistic, plausible distractors;
- avoid trick wording;
- avoid unnecessary cultural, socioeconomic, or tool-specific knowledge;
- avoid rewarding verbosity or confidence without evidence;
- use clear language appropriate to the construct being measured;
- avoid making English difficulty accidentally determine scores in non-language domains;
- distinguish a genuinely better answer from merely stylistic preference;
- have a documented rationale for the correct answer.

The assessment should favor scenario interpretation over isolated vocabulary recall.

## 11. Fairness and accessibility

Before production:

- review items for unnecessary language difficulty;
- review for cultural or socioeconomic bias;
- ensure reasonable accessibility accommodations do not replace the competency being tested;
- test mobile and desktop usability;
- ensure time pressure does not dominate constructs not intended to measure speed;
- provide clear instructions and practice/example formatting before the scored assessment.

## 12. Validation before real intake

The CEE must be piloted before it is treated as a stable placement instrument.

Pilot review should inspect:

- item difficulty;
- distractor quality;
- domain coverage;
- completion time;
- missing-response patterns;
- evaluator agreement on applied tasks;
- whether domain results actually predict which foundation support learners need;
- whether any item produces unfair or confusing results.

Poor items should be revised or removed. Cut scores should be validated against pilot evidence rather than chosen solely because they are numerically convenient.

## 13. Security boundary

The current frontend repository exposes client-side questions and answer keys and is suitable only for simulation and QA.

Before real intake, production must move to protected server-side systems for:

- item selection;
- answer keys;
- scoring;
- invitation validation;
- authoritative timing;
- submissions;
- evaluator records;
- pathway decisions.

Production should use a larger reviewed item bank with controlled randomization or equivalent forms so a leaked form does not invalidate the assessment.

## 14. External design references

The Cognita assessment model is informed by established competency-design principles, including:

- UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students — human-centred mindset, AI ethics, AI techniques/applications, and AI system design;
- European Commission Joint Research Centre DigComp — information/data literacy, communication, content creation, safety, and problem solving;
- OECD evidence-centred assessment design principles — define constructs, identify evidence, design tasks, assemble and validate the instrument.

These frameworks inform construct coverage. Cognita remains responsible for validating its own instrument for its intended learners and training decisions.
