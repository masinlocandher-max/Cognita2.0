1. INSTITUTIONAL STATUS AND EDUCATIONAL DISCLAIMER
======================================================================

1.1 Institutional Identity
Cognita Institute is an independent private training center providing non-degree learning, continuing education, enrichment, skills development, and professional development programs.

The use of the name "Institute" forms part of Cognita Institute's institutional identity. It does not, by itself, represent or imply government recognition, accreditation, licensing, endorsement, affiliation, or authorization.

1.2 No Implied Government Recognition
Unless expressly stated for a specific program and supported by valid official authority, Cognita Institute does not represent itself as:
- a CHED-recognized higher education institution;
- a DepEd-recognized basic education school;
- a TESDA-registered TVET institution or provider for a particular program;
- a PRC-licensed professional school or review provider;
- a government agency; or
- an institution authorized to grant government-recognized academic degrees, professional licenses, civil service eligibility, National Certificates, Certificates of Competency, or equivalent regulated credentials.

1.3 Regulated Programs
Where a program, qualification, or educational activity is subject to registration, accreditation, licensing, recognition, or other government authorization under Philippine law, Cognita Institute shall not advertise, enroll learners into, or represent that program as recognized or registered unless the required authority has been obtained.

Any regulatory status applicable to a particular program must be stated separately and specifically on the official program page, together with accurate details of the issuing authority where appropriate.

1.4 Cognita Entrance Examination
The Cognita Entrance Examination (CEE) is an internal admissions and placement assessment administered solely for Cognita Institute purposes.

Passing the CEE means that an applicant has satisfied Cognita Institute's internal admission requirements for the applicable program. The CEE is not a government examination, professional licensure examination, civil service examination, national competency assessment, academic equivalency examination, or government-recognized entrance examination unless an external authority expressly provides otherwise.

1.5 Certificates
Unless expressly stated otherwise for an authorized program, Cognita certificates are private institutional credentials documenting participation, completion, achievement, or performance within a Cognita program.

A Cognita Certificate of Completion or Certificate of Achievement is not:
- an academic degree;
- a CHED-recognized credential;
- a DepEd-recognized basic education credential;
- a TESDA National Certificate or Certificate of Competency;
- a professional license;
- a civil service eligibility;
- a government-issued certification; or
- an automatic academic, employment, or professional equivalency.

1.6 Outcomes
Completion of a Cognita program does not guarantee employment, promotion, income, business success, admission to another institution, academic credit, government eligibility, professional licensing, or acceptance by an employer or third party.

4. ADMISSIONS POLICY
======================================================================

4.1 Admissions Principle
Admission to Cognita Institute is based on eligibility, program requirements, readiness, available capacity, and compliance with the admissions process.

4.2 Standard Admissions Flow
Unless a program states otherwise, Cognita follows this process:

Step 1: Applicant completes registration/application.
Step 2: Cognita reviews the submitted application.
Step 3: Approved applicants receive a CEE invitation by email.
Step 4: Applicant completes the Cognita Entrance Examination within the stated conditions.
Step 5: Cognita communicates a pass, fail, placement, or further-review decision.
Step 6: Eligible applicants choose an available program.
Step 7: Applicant reviews and accepts enrollment policies.
Step 8: Applicant pays the applicable program fees.
Step 9: Enrolled learner creates or activates a student account.
Step 10: Learner receives access to the Cognita learning application.

4.3 Application Information
Applicants must provide accurate, complete, and current information. Cognita may request clarification or supporting documents reasonably necessary to evaluate eligibility.

4.4 Admission Decisions
Admission is not automatic.

Cognita may:
- approve an application;
- deny an application;
- request additional information;
- require the CEE;
- place an applicant into an appropriate level;
- impose reasonable prerequisite conditions; or
- defer admission when a program is unavailable.

4.5 Fair Treatment
Admissions decisions shall not be based on arbitrary, abusive, or unlawful discrimination.

4.6 False or Misleading Information
Materially false information, fraudulent documents, impersonation, or deliberate concealment may result in:
- application denial;
- cancellation of admission;
- enrollment termination; or
- certificate revocation if fraud is discovered after completion.

4.7 CEE Requirement
Where the CEE is required, the applicant must satisfy the CEE rules before final admission.

4.8 Admission Validity
An admission offer may be valid only for the period stated in the offer. Cognita may require reapplication or reassessment after expiration.

4.9 No Guarantee
Application submission, payment of an application fee, or invitation to take the CEE does not guarantee admission.

5. COGNITA ENTRANCE EXAMINATION AND ASSESSMENT POLICY
======================================================================

5.1 Purpose
The Cognita Entrance Examination is an internal assessment intended to evaluate readiness, placement, and eligibility for Cognita programs.

5.2 Access
CEE access is issued only to the authorized applicant and may not be shared or transferred.

5.3 Identity
The person taking the CEE must be the applicant whose name appears in the application.

5.4 Timer
The CEE may be subject to a fixed time limit. The timer begins as stated on the examination screen and continues according to the examination rules.

5.5 Permitted and Prohibited Resources
Before the examination begins, Cognita will state whether notes, calculators, external websites, AI tools, reference materials, or other resources are permitted.

Anything not expressly permitted for a closed assessment must be treated as prohibited.

5.6 Artificial Intelligence
Unless the examination instructions expressly permit it, use of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot, AI search, generative AI, or another AI assistance system during the CEE is prohibited.

5.7 Integrity Guardrails
Cognita may use proportionate technical and procedural controls to protect assessment integrity.

These may include:
- timers;
- attempt limits;
- randomized questions;
- activity logging;
- authentication;
- browser-session controls;
- network/device indicators;
- question banks;
- post-exam integrity review; and
- manual review of suspicious activity.

Any privacy-sensitive monitoring must be disclosed before the assessment.

5.8 Technical Failure
If an applicant experiences a material technical problem, the applicant must report it as soon as reasonably possible through the official support channel.

Cognita may review technical logs before granting a restart or replacement attempt.

5.9 Retakes
Retake eligibility, waiting periods, number of attempts, and any applicable retake fee must be disclosed before enrollment or examination.

Unless otherwise stated by a program:
- retakes are not automatic;
- Cognita may require a waiting period; and
- integrity violations may result in longer restrictions or ineligibility.

5.10 Invalidated Results
Results may be invalidated where there is credible evidence of:
- impersonation;
- prohibited assistance;
- answer sharing;
- unauthorized AI use;
- tampering;
- multiple-person participation;
- use of leaked assessment materials; or
- material violation of examination rules.

5.11 Result Review
An applicant may request review of a result or integrity decision through the Appeals Policy.

5.12 No External Equivalency
CEE scores have no independent academic, government, licensing, or professional equivalency outside Cognita unless expressly recognized by another authority.

8. ACADEMIC AND ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY POLICY
======================================================================

8.1 Principle
Cognita evaluates the learner's own knowledge, skills, judgment, and work except where collaboration or AI assistance is expressly permitted.

8.2 Academic Misconduct
Misconduct may include:
- plagiarism;
- copying another learner's work;
- unauthorized collaboration;
- impersonation;
- contract cheating;
- unauthorized AI use;
- fabrication of sources or results;
- falsification of documents;
- sharing assessment answers;
- acquiring leaked examination materials;
- bypassing assessment controls;
- manipulating technical systems; or
- submitting another person's work as one's own.

8.3 AI and Integrity
AI use is governed by both this policy and the AI Use Policy.

Where an assessment is marked "No AI," any use of generative AI to answer, rewrite, solve, summarize, or materially produce assessed work is prohibited unless a specific accommodation or exception has been approved.

8.4 Investigation
Cognita may investigate credible integrity concerns using:
- submitted work;
- platform logs;
- assessment records;
- comparison evidence;
- evaluator observations;
- learner explanation; and
- other lawful evidence.

AI-detection tools alone should not be treated as conclusive proof of misconduct.

8.5 Possible Outcomes
Depending on severity, outcomes may include:
- warning;
- required resubmission;
- score reduction;
- assessment failure;
- module failure;
- temporary suspension;
- invalidated CEE result;
- removal from the program;
- certificate withholding;
- certificate revocation in cases of proven fraud; or
- permanent ineligibility in exceptional serious cases.

8.6 Fair Process
Before a serious sanction, Cognita should provide the learner with the substance of the allegation and a reasonable opportunity to respond.

8.7 Appeals
Serious academic-integrity decisions may be appealed under the Complaints, Grievance, and Appeals Policy.

9. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE USE POLICY
======================================================================

9.1 Purpose
Cognita teaches learners to use AI intelligently, ethically, and critically. AI is a learning tool, not a substitute for demonstrated competence.

Every assessment or activity should be classified as one of the following:

A. AI ENCOURAGED
AI may be used as part of the learning objective.

B. AI ALLOWED WITH DISCLOSURE
AI may be used, but the learner must disclose material assistance and remain responsible for accuracy.

A learner remains responsible for:
- factual accuracy;
- citations;
- originality;
- confidentiality;
- lawful use;
- bias awareness;
- safety;

- fabricate references;
- generate fraudulent documents;
- bypass security controls;
- harass or threaten;
- process information unlawfully; or

Learners must not upload private Cognita materials, assessment questions, confidential business information, personal information, sensitive personal information, or another person's data into external AI systems unless specifically authorized and lawful.

9.6 Disclosure

- the AI tool used;

- the material generated or assisted;

9.8 Changes

- serious assessment misconduct; or

23. INTERNAL DATA SECURITY AND BREACH RESPONSE POLICY
NOT FOR PUBLIC WEBSITE UNLESS COGNITA CHOOSES TO PUBLISH IT
======================================================================

23.1 Security Governance
Cognita shall identify the person or role responsible for privacy and information security.

23.2 Access Control
Staff access shall be limited according to role and necessity.

23.3 Authentication
Administrative and sensitive systems should use strong authentication and multi-factor authentication where supported.

23.4 Production Data
Real learner data must not be copied into insecure testing environments without authorization and safeguards.

23.5 Secrets
API keys, passwords, service-role credentials, database secrets, signing keys, and payment secrets must never be embedded in public frontend code or repositories.

23.6 Incident Definition
A security incident includes suspected:
- unauthorized access;
- data loss;
- credential compromise;
- malware;
- exposed database;
- leaked documents;
- accidental disclosure;
- unauthorized alteration; or
- destruction of personal data.

23.7 Response
When an incident is detected:
1. Preserve relevant evidence.
2. Contain the incident.
3. Revoke compromised credentials.
4. Determine affected systems and data.
5. Assess risk to data subjects.
6. Escalate to the privacy/security lead.
7. Document actions taken.
8. Notify affected persons and/or the National Privacy Commission where required by applicable law.
9. Correct the root cause.
10. Conduct a post-incident review.

23.8 Vendors
Cognita shall review privacy and security risks before giving service providers access to learner data.

24. INTERNAL ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY OPERATING STANDARD
======================================================================

24.1 Objective
Integrity controls must be effective enough to protect assessment validity while remaining proportionate to privacy risk.

24.2 Preferred Controls
Cognita should prefer lower-intrusion controls first:
- authenticated access;
- timed assessments;
- question randomization;
- large item banks;
- one-question-at-a-time display where appropriate;
- attempt limits;
- plagiarism review;
- oral verification for suspicious high-stakes submissions;
- project-based assessments;
- manual evaluator review;
- versioned questions; and
- activity logs.

24.3 High-Intrusion Controls
Webcam proctoring, facial recognition, biometric verification, continuous screen recording, microphone recording, invasive browser monitoring, or similar measures require separate approval after:
- necessity assessment;
- proportionality assessment;
- privacy review;
- security review;
- retention decision;
- vendor review; and
- clear applicant disclosure.

24.4 No Automatic Guilt
A technical anomaly alone is not proof of cheating.
