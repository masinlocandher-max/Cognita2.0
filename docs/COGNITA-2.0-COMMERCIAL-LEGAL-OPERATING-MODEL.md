# Cognita 2.0 — Commercial, Product and Legal Operating Model

Status: CANONICAL
Effective: 2026-09-28

This document controls Cognita's business model, CEE routing logic, learning-mode architecture, conversion rules, and legal positioning. Where an older document conflicts with this file or docs/COGNITA-2.0-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md, the older rule is superseded.

## 1. Number-one objective

Cognita is a business.

Primary objective: **generate sustainable revenue and profit.**

Educational quality, learner outcomes, trust, compliance, accessibility, and credibility are operating requirements because they improve conversion, retention, referrals, renewals, pricing power, institutional sales, and long-term business value.

Commercial hierarchy:
1. Sustainable revenue and profit
2. Product-market fit
3. Customer acquisition and conversion
4. Revenue per learner and institutional contract value
5. Retention, progression, upsell, renewal, and referrals
6. Strong learner outcomes and demonstrable value
7. Brand trust, legal compliance, and long-term credibility
8. Scalable delivery and healthy margins

## 2. Core customer journey

**Create Account → CEE → Learner Profile → Gap Analysis → Personalized Pathway → Learning Mode Recommendation → Paid Offer → Training → Mastery → Portfolio Evidence → Verified Competency Record → Next Paid Path**

Cognita must not behave primarily like a public course catalog.

The default experience is:
**Based on your CEE, this is the path Cognita recommends for you.**

Learners may explore alternatives where academically eligible, but Cognita recommends first.

## 3. CEE definition

Preferred public name:
**Cognita Entry & Competency Evaluation (CEE)**

CEE is the academic routing engine and commercial recommendation engine.

It determines:
- current demonstrated competency
- strongest capabilities
- critical gaps
- technical readiness
- learning-readiness/support needs
- goals and practical constraints
- recommended depth pathway
- recommended specialization
- competencies that may be skipped
- competencies that must be mastered
- recommended learning mode
- support intensity
- recommended paid offer

## 4. CEE four-layer profile

### Competency Diagnostic
Measures AI literacy, applied AI problem solving, prompt/instruction design, output verification, research/evidence judgment, privacy/security/responsible use, digital/computational literacy, workflow/automation reasoning, hallucination detection, governance, and professional judgment.

### Technical Readiness
Measures digital fluency, computational thinking, programming readiness, systems reasoning, and readiness for technical tools.

### Learner Readiness & Support Profile
This is a non-clinical educational readiness profile, not a psychological or mental-health diagnosis.

It may assess self-regulation, persistence, study consistency, accountability needs, response to failure, confidence calibration, ambiguity tolerance, willingness to experiment, problem-solving orientation, digital confidence, and preference for private/public or synchronous/asynchronous participation.

Never label a learner lazy, weak, unintelligent, unstable, clinically impaired, or diagnose a mental-health or psychological condition.

Translate results into support recommendations such as:
- Strong fit for self-paced learning
- Likely to benefit from weekly accountability
- Needs shorter milestones and more frequent feedback
- May perform better with private/asynchronous participation
- Confidence-calibration gap indicates additional verification practice

### Goal & Constraint Profile
Capture career/business goal, target role/use case, desired specialization, available study time, device access, internet reliability, language preference, schedule, prior experience, and practical budget constraints where commercially appropriate.

## 5. Depth pathways

1. **AI Literacy** — understand, evaluate, and use AI responsibly.
2. **Applied AI** — apply AI effectively in professional, business, education, government, research, and creative workflows.
3. **AI Builder** — design automations, applications, agents, APIs, structured workflows, and connected systems.
4. **AI Engineering** — programming, machine-learning foundations, system architecture, evaluation, deployment, reliability, and MLOps.

Not every learner starts at Level 1 and not every learner needs Level 4.

## 6. Learning modes

### Self-Paced
Lower-touch paid option for learners who can work independently. Same competency standards, flexible scheduling, AI-assisted feedback where implemented, assessments, remediation, projects, and credential evidence.

### Guided
Premium B2C route for learners who benefit from structure, checkpoints, accountability, instructor/mentor feedback, scheduled support, community/cohort features, and capstone review.

### Cognita for Organizations
Baseline assessment → competency-gap analysis → customized pathway → cohort training → verification → organization report → renewal/expansion.

## 7. Mastery architecture

**Learn → Do → Submit/Perform → Evaluate → Explain → Remediate → Retry → Master**

Watching content or spending time in the platform does not establish competence.

Major units should generate evidence such as research files, workflows, policies, automations, campaign systems, prototypes, data analyses, business cases, or capstones.

## 8. Credential model

Cognita credentials represent demonstrated evidence, not seat time.

The long-term Cognita Verified Competency Record should be capable of showing:
- CEE baseline
- competencies attempted
- competencies mastered
- evidence
- projects/capstone
- final verified level
- areas not assessed

## 9. Commercial model

Revenue can come from:
- CEE or premium assessment access where approved
- Self-Paced enrollment
- Guided premium enrollment
- advanced/specialized pathways
- reassessment
- portfolio/capstone review
- proprietary credentialing and verification
- continuing education
- institutional training contracts
- customized organization pathways
- curriculum/material licensing
- lawful organization dashboards/reporting
- separate commercialization agreements for learner-created work

Value ladder:
**Entry/CEE → Personalized Paid Path → Advanced/Specialized Path → Guided/Premium Support → Competency Verification → Continuing Education → Institutional/Enterprise Training**

Free or low-cost elements must have a defined commercial job such as acquisition, qualification, trust building, conversion, or reactivation.

Track:
- visitor-to-account conversion
- account-to-CEE completion
- CEE-to-paid conversion
- recommendation acceptance rate
- revenue per learner
- gross margin
- completion-to-upsell rate
- repeat purchase rate
- refund rate
- referral rate
- institutional contract value
- renewal/expansion rate
- customer lifetime value

## 11. Website hook and conversion language

Primary hook:
**Stop taking AI courses you may not need.**

Support:
**Take the CEE. Discover your AI level. Get the learning path built for you.**

Long-term positioning:
**Don't just learn AI. Prove you can use it.**

B2B:
**Don't train everyone the same. Measure the gaps. Train what matters. Verify the capability.**

Use persuasive but truthful psychology: personalization, curiosity, uncertainty reduction, progress framing, commitment, and truthful loss aversion.

Do not use fabricated scarcity, fake countdowns, invented outcomes, hidden charges, misleading guarantees, or deliberately alarming learner labels.

## 12. Pricing and recommendation integrity

The system may recommend a higher-priced Guided pathway when CEE evidence genuinely indicates that additional structure, accountability, feedback, or facilitation is likely to improve the learner's outcome.

Do not secretly personalize prices based on inferred vulnerability, anxiety, desperation, wealth, or willingness to pay.

Standardize pricing by product. Personalize the recommendation.

## 13. Private-training legal positioning

Cognita should be publicly positioned as a:
**private, non-degree professional training and competency-development provider.**

Do not claim CHED recognition, DepEd school recognition, TESDA registration/accreditation, TESDA National Certificates or Certificates of Competency, PRC recognition/licensure, government academic credit, or government approval unless the applicable authority has actually been obtained and is verifiable for the specific program.

Before any program that may fall within regulated TVET launches, obtain a formal legal/compliance determination and complete required registration where applicable.

## 14. CEE psychological/psychometric boundary

Do not market the readiness section as a psychological examination, clinical psychological assessment, mental-health assessment, or regulated psychometric service unless properly licensed professionals, instruments, and procedures are used.

Use:
**Learner Readiness & Support Profile**

Its purpose is educational routing and support.

## 15. Privacy, profiling and automated recommendations

Before production:
- designate the required privacy/DPO function
- conduct a Privacy Impact Assessment
- map lawful bases
- register relevant data-processing/profiling systems where required
- provide clear Privacy Notice and CEE profiling disclosure
- provide meaningful information about the logic and consequences of profiling
- provide a review/challenge route for materially incorrect recommendations
- define retention/deletion schedules
- implement access control, logging, breach response, vendor safeguards, and data minimization

## 16. Consumer and e-commerce controls

Before payment clearly disclose:
- legal/trade name
- business address when finalized
- valid email/contact channel
- service description
- price
- billing cadence
- access period
- material limitations
- refund/cancellation terms
- complaint/redress channel
- transaction confirmation/receipt

## 17. Learner work and IP

Learners retain ownership of original work unless a separate written agreement states otherwise.

Ordinary submission grants Cognita only the rights necessary to store, evaluate, annotate, process, conduct integrity checks, administer training, and verify credentials.

Commercial sale, licensing, publication, incubation, marketplace use, or other commercial exploitation of learner-owned work requires a separate written agreement defining ownership/license, exclusivity, attribution, compensation/revenue share, derivative rights, confidentiality, taxes, warranties, and termination.

## 18. Adults-first launch

Recommended V1 direct-to-consumer eligibility: 18+.

A minors product requires a separate privacy, safeguarding, consent, and age-appropriate design review.

## 19. Frontend-only constraint

The repository remains frontend-only until production infrastructure is explicitly approved and implemented.

Do not pretend local simulations are live account creation, CEE submission, payment, authentication, learner records, or credential issuance.

The product architecture is account-first, but production account creation must not be represented as live until secure backend identity and authorization exist.

## 20. Superseded beliefs

The following are superseded:
- application-first/human-review-first admission as the universal front door
- invitation-only CEE as the core commercial model
- CEE primarily as pass/fail admission gate
- learners choosing a generic course catalog before recommendation
- prompt engineering as the flagship center of Cognita
- seat time/completion as evidence of competence
- tool-specific mastery as competence
- certificates as the primary product
